FAILURE LAB - 22 LSTM / GRU
Reproduce -> diagnose -> fix -> report.
================================================================================

LAB 1 - THE FORGET-GATE AMNESIA
  LSTM trained on long sequences learns the first 50 steps then "forgets"
  and outputs noise for the rest.
  Tasks: (1) reproduce with a long dependency task (signal at step 5,
  answer at step 200); (2) inspect forget-gate values over training -
  if they start near 0, the cell erases memory immediately; (3) fix
  with forget-gate bias init ~1 and orthogonal recurrent init; (4)
  report loss over sequence positions before/after.

LAB 2 - GRU vs LSTM: THE HONEST COMPARISON
  Claim: "GRU is always better because fewer parameters." On your data
  LSTM beats GRU by 2% - or vice versa.
  Tasks: (1) build BOTH with matched capacity (units, layers, budget);
  (2) run 5 seeds and report mean + spread, not one lucky run; (3)
  conclude which wins on YOUR data and argue why (sequence length,
  data size, task type); (4) state when you would default to each.

LAB 3 - THE STACKED OVERFIT
  3-layer bidirectional LSTM with 512 units on 5k samples: train
  accuracy 99%, val 60%.
  Tasks: (1) plot curves; (2) reduce capacity (layers, units,
  bidirectional->unidirectional) and add recurrent dropout; (3) compare
  5 configurations on the SAME val split; (4) pick the smallest model
  within 1% of the best and justify.

LAB 4 - THE BIDIRECTIONAL LEAK
  Bidirectional LSTM on a forecasting task where t+1 must be predicted
  from the past only: validation great, live predictions terrible.
  Tasks: (1) reproduce with the correct task framing (causal vs
  non-causal); (2) show that the backward pass saw the future; (3) fix
  with a causal (unidirectional or masked) model; (4) evaluate with a
  walk-forward protocol and report the honest error.

LAB 5 - THE LONG-SEQUENCE OOM
  LSTM on 10k-step sequences: CUDA OOM at batch 8.
  Tasks: (1) explain memory growth with sequence length (BPTT stores
  activations per step); (2) fix with truncated BPTT (tbptt) or
  gradient checkpointing or smaller batch; (3) quantify max batch size
  before/after on your hardware; (4) discuss when truncation biases
  learning and how to bound it.

LAB 6 - THE STUBBORN LSTM
  LSTM refuses to beat a persistence baseline on a noisy series, even
  after tuning.
  Tasks: (1) reproduce baseline vs LSTM; (2) diagnose: is the signal
  real (ACF of the series)? is the window right? is MSE the wrong loss
  (quantile)?; (3) fix the FEATURES or framing (lags, exogenous
  inputs, quantile loss) rather than the LSTM; (4) conclude when the
  honest answer is "use the naive baseline / a simpler model".
