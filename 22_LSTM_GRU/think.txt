THINK.TXT - 22 LSTM / GRU
================================================================================

1. The cell state c_t is the LSTM's highway for gradients. Trace
   dL/dc_t through the forget gate and show why the additive path lets
   gradients survive long sequences where a plain RNN's multiplicative
   path dies.
2. Why does the forget gate being initialized near 1 matter more than
   the input gate's init? Connect this to gradient flow at t=0 of
   training.
3. GRU merges the cell and hidden state and drops the output gate.
   Count parameters and argue when the smaller GRU beats LSTM and when
   LSTM's extra gate pays off.
4. "LSTM gates are a soft attention over memory." Defend this view,
   then say where it breaks (gates are input-dependent scalars, not a
   content-addressable lookup).
5. A 3-layer stacked LSTM with 256 units overfits your 5k-sample
   dataset. Rank fixes: fewer layers, more dropout, smaller hidden
   size, regularization, pretraining/embeddings - and justify with the
   capacity/regularization trade-off.
6. Bidirectional LSTM: doubles capacity and sees the future. When is
   seeing the future legitimate (NLP classification) vs leakage (time
   series forecasting)? Formalize the rule.
7. Your LSTM forecaster's test loss is worse than naive persistence on
   a noisy series. Is the LSTM broken? Argue when a persistence
   baseline SHOULD win and what it says about the signal-to-noise
   ratio.
8. Teacher forcing during training masks errors that appear at
   inference (exposure bias). Explain the failure mode and how
   scheduled sampling or curriculum approaches address it.
9. An LSTM for machine translation needs attention to beat a 30-token
   bottleneck. Explain why a fixed-size context vector loses
   information regardless of LSTM quality - the motivation for
   attention (Module 25).
10. Numerical: a GRU has 3 gates, hidden 128, input 64. Compute the
    parameter count of the recurrent cell (ignore bias nuances, state
    the formula). Compare with LSTM for the same sizes.
11. Your LSTM trains fine but CUDA OOMs during long sequences. Explain
    why memory grows with sequence length (BPTT stores activations per
    step) and the fixes: truncated BPTT, gradient checkpointing,
    smaller batch.
12. OPEN PROBLEM: gated RNNs have been largely replaced by attention
    and state-space models for language, yet remain strong for some
    streaming/online tasks. Characterize precisely the task properties
    where recurrence still wins and where it cannot compete.
