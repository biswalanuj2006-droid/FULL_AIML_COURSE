================================================================================
THINK.TXT - 21 RNN
================================================================================

1. An RNN shares weights across time. Derive why that sharing is what
   makes BPTT's gradient a product over time steps, and show how
   gradient norms behave with eigenvalues of the recurrent weight
   matrix (the vanishing/exploding mechanism).
2. Why can't an RNN with sigmoid/tanh remember a fact from 50 steps
   back even with lots of capacity? Separate the optimization story
   (vanishing gradients) from the capacity story (information
   bottleneck).
3. RNNs process sequences one step at a time - no parallelism across
   time. Quantify the training-time cost vs a Transformer on a
   512-token sequence and explain why this killed plain RNNs at scale.
4. BPTT truncation (tbptt): you backprop through only k steps. What
   bias does that introduce and how do you choose k for a 10,000-step
   sequence?
5. Exploding gradients: gradient clipping helps. Why does clipping by
   NORM work better than clipping each element? What does the clip
   value do to the effective step?
6. Your char-level RNN produces fluent but nonsense text. Diagnose:
   is it underfitting (capacity), overfitting (memorized), or a
   decoding problem? What does the training/validation loss gap tell
   you?
7. Sequence tasks: language modeling, translation, speech, and time
   series each need different input/output alignments (many-to-many,
   seq2seq, teacher forcing). For each, sketch the RNN architecture
   and where teacher forcing helps/hurts.
8. When would you still choose an RNN over a Transformer in 2026?
   Give two genuine cases (long sequences with linear memory
   preference, small-data regimes, online/streaming) and defend them.
9. A stock-price LSTM overfits training but looks great in backtests.
   Explain what temporal leakage + random split does, and how walk-
   forward evaluation changes the conclusion.
10. Initialization of the recurrent weight matrix: orthogonal init
    helps RNNs. Explain why (spectral radius) and what happens if the
    spectral radius > 1.
11. OPEN PROBLEM: whether recurrence or attention is the "right"
    inductive bias for sequence modeling remains an active question
    (state-space models, linear attention). Argue what an ideal
    sequence layer must satisfy and which existing layer comes closest.
