================================================================================
THINK - 59 RECOMMENDER SYSTEMS (hard reasoning)
================================================================================
Answer with derivations or arguments, not one-liners. Items marked OPEN
PROBLEM are genuinely unsolved; say what evidence would settle them.

1. Derive the ALS update for implicit feedback from the confidence
   objective. Why can the unobserved pairs (confidence 1, p=0) still be
   summed efficiently without materializing all n_user x n_item pairs?

2. Matrix factorization minimizes squared error on observed ratings.
   Explain precisely why minimizing RMSE is NOT the same as optimizing
   NDCG, and give a ratings distribution where the two disagree about
   which of two models is better.

3. A rating of 1 (dislike) and a missing rating are treated the same by
   plain MF with mean-imputation, but are completely different signals.
   Formalize the difference and design an objective that respects it.

4. Prove/argue: under the rich-get-richer loop, training on logged
   policy data maximizes a proxy that diverges from true user
   satisfaction. Write the feedback equation p_t+1 = f(p_t) and analyze
   its fixed points.

5. Position bias: click probability depends on rank. Write the
   generative model click ~ Bernoulli(sigmoid(relevance - rank_penalty))
   and derive why a naive model trained on clicks learns rank, not
   relevance. What does the "position as a feature, neutralize at
   scoring" fix actually compute?

6. Cold start for a new item with zero interactions: compare (a) item-
   metadata similarity, (b) two-tower with content features, (c) hybrid
   MF. Which has the best worst-case behavior and why?

7. Two-tower models trained with in-batch negatives suffer from popular-
   item bias (negatives are too easy). Derive why sampled softmax with
   logQ correction fixes the biased gradient, and the failure when the
   proposal Q is wrong.

8. A/B test: new model wins CTR by 1% (p < 0.01, 10M impressions).
   Should you launch? Give the arguments for and against beyond
   statistical significance (long-term value, novelty effect, metric
   gaming).

9. Explain why time-split evaluation can still be optimistic when the
   model uses user-history features: trace how a user's future action
   can leak into a "past" feature at scoring time.

10. (OPEN PROBLEM) Offline-to-online gap: logged data is generated by
    the deployed policy, so every offline evaluation is biased by that
    policy. Propose an evaluation protocol and state precisely the
    conditions under which you would trust it to predict an online A/B.

11. (OPEN PROBLEM) Diversity vs accuracy: ranking by expected engagement
    provably collapses the catalog (repeated exposure -> repeated
    engagement). Formalize the optimization with an exposure-constraint
    and discuss why its solution depends on knowing true relevance.

12. Design a recommender for a domain where the item is the user's own
    past content (e.g. "people like you also wrote"). Which parts of
    the standard stack break, and how do you restructure candidate
    generation?
