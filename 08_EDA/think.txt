THINK.TXT - 08 EDA
================================================================================

1. EDA is called "exploratory", yet you are asked to produce a report with
   conclusions. Where is the line between exploring and p-hacking? Define
   rules that keep your EDA honest.
2. You find a correlation of 0.9 between two features. List five distinct
   explanations (causation, confounding, collinearity, composition,
   measurement) and the analysis that would discriminate each.
3. Simpson's paradox: explain it with a concrete dataset and show how a
   correct segmentation flips a conclusion. When would you still report the
   unsegmented number?
4. Every EDA chart you make implicitly assumes something about the data
   generating process. For a histogram, a scatter, and a boxplot, state the
   assumption and when it silently lies.
5. A feature has 30% missing values that correlate perfectly with the
   target being positive. Your EDA shows this. What are the modeling
   implications and what would you recommend to the team?
6. You only have time for 5 plots before modeling 50 features. Choose the
   5, justify each, and explain what failure each one specifically guards
   against.
7. Outliers in EDA: a domain expert says "that's just how the data is",
   a statistician says "remove them", a modeler says "keep them".
   Construct the argument each is making and decide with evidence.
8. Your EDA used 100% of the data and informed feature engineering, model
   choice, and thresholds. A colleague says this invalidates your test
   set. Argue both sides precisely - what does "using" the test set
   mean and where is the actual boundary?
9. Leakage detection by EDA: what specific plots and summaries would
   reveal that a feature contains the future or the answer? Give three
   concrete recipes.
10. You compare two segments and find the difference "significant".
    The dataset has 40 columns and you checked 100 comparisons. Recompute
    your confidence in the finding and explain the correction needed.
11. OPEN PROBLEM: How much EDA is too much? Formalize the point at which
    additional exploration stops improving model decisions and starts
    overfitting your own judgment to the sample. Propose a measurable
    criterion.
