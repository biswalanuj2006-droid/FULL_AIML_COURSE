THINK.TXT - 18 ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORKS
================================================================================

1. A single neuron with a sigmoid is logistic regression. Prove it.
   Then argue what a hidden layer actually adds that a wider input
   cannot.
2. Universal approximation says an MLP with one hidden layer can
   approximate any continuous function. Yet depth wins in practice.
   Reconcile: what does the theorem NOT say (width, learnability,
   sample complexity)?
3. All-zero weights break learning; random init works. Explain
   precisely why symmetry makes gradient descent stuck, and what
   symmetry-breaking ReLU has that sigmoid lacks (dead neurons aside).
4. The sigmoid saturates and its derivative -> 0. Trace how a 10-layer
   sigmoid net's gradients behave and why that is worse than ReLU's
   sparsity. Where does ReLU fail instead (dying ReLU)?
5. Softmax + cross-entropy gives gradients (p - y). Derive this and
   explain why MSE at the output trains 10-100x slower for
   classification (hint: what happens to dMSE/dz when p is wrong but
   sigmoid flattens?).
6. Batch size 1 vs 1024: same model, same lr. Sketch both loss curves
   and explain the noise-bias trade-off. Why does the optimal lr scale
   with batch size (linear scaling rule)?
7. Your train loss decreases but validation loss rises from epoch 3.
   Rank the interventions (dropout, weight decay, early stopping,
   more data, smaller model) by expected effect and justify the order.
8. Weight initialization: what does He vs Xavier vs zero mean for
   forward variance and backward variance? Derive the condition that
   He init satisfies for ReLU.
9. A 2-layer net needs 5 hidden units for your task, a 10-layer net
   with 50 units per layer overfits catastrophically. Construct the
   learning-curve argument for why the deep net is worse and when deep
   nets are actually better (data scale, hierarchy).
10. Numerical gradient checks pass at 1e-7 but training diverges.
    Where does the analytic/numeric agreement break down (loss
    landscape, floating point, batch norm)?
11. Your net memorizes 100% of training but 50% of test on a dataset
    with 1,000 samples and 10,000 features. Is it capacity, data, or
    optimization? Design the experiment that distinguishes all three.
12. A colleague removes the bias terms "to simplify" and accuracy drops
    by 3%. Explain mathematically what biases buy (decision boundary
    through the origin) and when removing them is harmless.
13. OPEN PROBLEM: why gradient descent finds low-error solutions on
    overparameterized nets (the "loss landscape" question) lacks a
    complete theory. State what is known (implicit regularization of
    GD/SGD) and what remains open.
