================================================================================
REAL PROBLEM - 10 REGRESSION
================================================================================

PROBLEM A: "House-price model: R2 0.96 in validation, predictions in
production are nonsense (negative prices, wild swings)."
WHY IT HAPPENS
  1. Look for target leakage: a feature like "sale_price_2" from a
     joined table, or a future transaction date.
  2. Check extrapolation: validation covered a normal price range but
     production asks about mansions - linear models extrapolate
     horribly.
  3. Check that the SAME preprocessing (scaler fit on train) is applied
     at serving - if serving re-fits the scaler on one row, mean/std
     are garbage.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Ablate features; dump the whole pipeline (not the model alone) and
     reuse it at serving.
  2. Clip predictions to the training target range; flag out-of-range
     inputs instead of predicting blindly.
  3. Log input + prediction + model version; compare live feature
     ranges against training ranges.

PROBLEM B: "Two features are almost identical (correlation 0.999); my
coefficients are huge, opposite signs, and change every retrain."
WHY: multicollinearity - X^T X is nearly singular, so the inverse
explodes and the fit "splits" the effect arbitrarily between the twins.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Confirm with VIF > 10.
  2. Drop one twin, or use Ridge (shrinks both smoothly), or Lasso
     (picks one), or PCA.
  3. If you must keep both for business reasons, report their JOINT
     effect, never individual coefficients.

PROBLEM C: "RMSE looks great but the business complains about big
errors on small transactions."
WHY: RMSE is dominated by large values; errors on small transactions
are invisible in the aggregate.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Report MAE and MAPE alongside RMSE; slice errors by transaction
     size bucket.
  2. If small-value accuracy matters, switch loss to MAPE or log-target
     regression.
  3. Show a residual-vs-predicted plot; heteroscedasticity (errors
     growing with value) explains the complaint.

PROBLEM D: "Retraining weekly makes the model WORSE over time."
WHY: each retrain tunes against a slightly different validation sample;
you are selecting on noise (the selection trap) or the world is
drifting and you are chasing it with too-small windows.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Fix a single evaluation protocol (fixed walk-forward folds) and
     only ship if the new model beats the current one on that protocol.
  2. Keep a champion/challenger: challenger must beat champion on the
     SAME folds by a margin before rollout.
  3. If drift is real, retrain on a rolling window sized by the drift
     speed - measure, don't guess.
