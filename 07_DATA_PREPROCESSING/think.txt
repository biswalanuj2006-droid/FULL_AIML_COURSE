THINK.TXT - 07 DATA PREPROCESSING
================================================================================

1. Imputation: you may use mean, median, mode, or a model. For each,
   describe a dataset where it is the RIGHT choice and one where it is
   catastrophically wrong (think about what "missing" means in each case).
2. Missing Not At Random (MNAR): the missingness itself is information.
   How would you encode "was missing" as a feature and when is that
   legitimate rather than leakage?
3. Outlier definition is circular: outliers are points that break your
   assumptions, but you need assumptions to find them. Give three concrete
   definitions (z-score, IQR, isolation forest) and what each assumes.
4. Trace step by step how scaling BEFORE a train/test split leaks, then
   explain why the leak is invisible in most metrics until deployment.
5. A column has 1,000 categories with 99% in a "long tail". Compare
   one-hot, ordinal, frequency, and target encoding for this column under
   each of: tree models, linear models, neural networks. Which leaks and
   under what CV setup?
6. You discover 12% duplicate rows in training data. Argue for and against
   removing them before you know the business context (fraud vs sensor
   logs vs survey responses).
7. Design a preprocessing pipeline that must run identically at training
   and serving time, where serving receives rows one at a time and
   training saw batches. Where do your transforms break, and how do you
   make them streaming-safe?
8. "The model performs great on validation, poorly in production."
   Suppose preprocessing is the culprit. Enumerate every preprocessing
   step that could differ between the two and how to detect each.
9. You have time to fix exactly one of: missing values, outliers, or
   label noise. Which, and how do you justify it with data?
10. Target encoding a high-cardinality column inside CV folds is safe, but
    the same encoding outside folds leaks. Derive WHY the fold boundary
    matters using a single example with 3 folds.
11. Synthetic data (SMOTE or generative) created before splitting: explain
    precisely how synthetic samples end up in both train and validation
    and why that inflates scores.
12. OPEN PROBLEM: There is no universally best missing-value strategy - it
    depends on the missingness mechanism, which is usually unobservable.
    Design a decision procedure that chooses a strategy from the data
    itself without assuming the mechanism. What are its failure modes?
