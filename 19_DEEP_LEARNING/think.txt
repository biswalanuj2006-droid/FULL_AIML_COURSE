THINK.TXT - 19 DEEP LEARNING
================================================================================

1. Adam works "out of the box" yet papers often report SGD+momentum
   generalizing better after tuning. Explain the mechanism (adaptive
   rates and generalization) and when you would deliberately choose
   each.
2. Batch normalization changes the loss landscape, not just the
   activations. Argue this with the Lipschitz-smoothness intuition and
   say why BN behaves differently at train vs eval (and the batch
   size dependency at inference).
3. Dropout is an ensemble of subnetworks AND a regularizer that changes
   the effective learning rate. Give both views and predict what
   happens when you forget to scale activations at test time.
4. Your test loss is 0.35, your friend's identical-looking model gets
   0.25. Name ten differences that could cause it, ranked by
   likelihood, and the one-line experiment that tests each.
5. Reproducibility: you set every seed and still get different results
   across two machines. Enumerate the sources (CUDA nondeterminism,
   data loading order, tf.data, float reduction order) and the
   practical policy for ML teams that need comparable runs.
6. Learning rate schedules: warmup then cosine decay helps large
   batches. Explain why warmup matters when Adam's second moment is
   still being estimated, and why cosine beats step decay for long
   runs.
7. Gradient accumulation vs larger batch: same effective batch, but
   BatchNorm statistics differ. When does accumulation NOT reproduce a
   big batch, and how do you compensate?
8. Mixed precision (FP16) trains 2x faster but can diverge. Explain
   loss scaling, why master weights are kept in FP32, and what the
   gradient underflow failure looks like.
9. You can only afford 10 GPU-hours. Argue whether to spend it on
   more epochs, a bigger model, a bigger batch with a schedule, or
   ensembling - with a decision rule that depends on your current
   overfit/underfit diagnosis.
10. Deep nets generalize despite memorizing training data. Construct
    the double-descent argument and say what it implies about
    "regularization = restraint" as a mental model.
11. Early stopping is "free" regularization. Prove it is equivalent to
    weight decay in a simplified setting, and explain why it is still
    the first callbacks people reach for.
12. Your validation curve is flat but train is still falling at epoch
    100. Is the model overfitting, underfitting, or done? How do you
    decide whether to continue, and what does the answer cost?
13. OPEN PROBLEM: the generalization gap between SGD and adaptive
    optimizers has no settled explanation. Survey the leading
    hypotheses and design the experiment that would discriminate them.
