================================================================================
THINK.TXT - 11 ANOMALY DETECTION
================================================================================

1. All anomaly detectors define "normal" implicitly. State the implicit
   definition of normality for z-score, LOF, Isolation Forest, and a
   trained autoencoder - then give one dataset where each definition is
   catastrophically wrong.
2. "Anomaly detection is density estimation at a specific quantile."
   Defend or attack this claim. What breaks the equivalence when the
   data has 100 dimensions?
3. Masking and swamping: explain both with one dataset. Why do robust
   estimators fix masking but introduce their own failure (breakdown
   point)? What is the breakdown point of the median vs the mean?
4. Isolation Forest scores depend on the random splits and the sampling
   of max_samples. Characterize what kinds of anomalies are systematically
   missed (thin regions along a feature where normal variance is large)
   and propose a modification.
5. You have labels for only the LAST month of fraud but 12 months of
   unlabeled history. Design the exact training/evaluation protocol that
   uses both without fooling yourself. Where does the "supervised"
   upgrade (module 09) start paying off?
6. A seasonal time series with an upward trend: walk through every
   failure mode of a naive rolling-z detector, then design the detector
   that handles trend + seasonality + spikes + regime shift with one
   alert budget. Which of those four phenomena can one threshold serve?
7. Distance concentration: in high dimensions all points are roughly
   equidistant. Show why kNN/LOF degrade; does Isolation Forest suffer
   the same way? (Think about what IF actually measures.)
8. Contamination is a parameter, but real anomaly rates drift over
   time. Design an adaptive threshold that tracks a changing anomaly
   rate without labels. What are its failure modes?
9. Novelty vs outlier: one-class methods distinguish "seen normal but
   rare" from "never seen". When does a rare-but-normal cluster get
   flagged, and how do you tell the difference operationally?
10. Autoencoders trained on contaminated data memorize the anomalies.
    Quantify when this happens (contamination fraction vs capacity) and
    design the training scheme that prevents it.
11. Every anomaly you catch is either a real event or a data-quality
    bug, and the two look identical at first. Propose features of the
    anomaly (persistence, affected columns, domain checks) that separate
    them.
12. OPEN PROBLEM: evaluation of unsupervised anomaly detection without
    any labels has no settled metric. Design one (or argue none can
    exist) and state exactly what it would measure and where it breaks.
