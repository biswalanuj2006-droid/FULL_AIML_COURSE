================================================================================
THINK.TXT - 06 ML FUNDAMENTALS
Difficult reasoning problems. Attempt them; do not look for single "correct"
answers. OPEN PROBLEM means no settled answer exists - argue with evidence.
================================================================================

1. A model gets 99% accuracy on validation but fails in production. List
   every plausible cause you can think of, ordered by likelihood, and for
   each: what evidence would confirm it and what fix you would try.
2. Explain overfitting to a non-technical stakeholder using ONLY a physical
   analogy. Then explain why that analogy breaks down mathematically.
3. "More data beats a better model." When is this true, when false, and how
   would you test it with finite compute?
4. You are given a dataset with 10 features but a senior engineer says to
   add 90 more synthetic features derived from them. Argue both sides with
   specific failure modes (optimization, generalization, leakage).
5. Train/validation/test: some practitioners carve the test set once and
   never touch it. Others prefer nested CV. Derive when each is correct and
   what each silently assumes about the future.
6. What exactly does a "label" mean when human annotators disagree 15% of
   the time? How does label noise change the ceiling of achievable
   accuracy, and how would you estimate that ceiling?
7. Is a model that memorizes but generalizes possible? Characterize the
   boundary between "memorization" and "learning" with examples (image
   duplicates, ID features, few-shot regimes).
8. A colleague fits StandardScaler on the full dataset "just to look at
   distributions" and reuses those stats later in the pipeline. Trace the
   exact path by which test information enters training.
9. Bias-variance: you can only reduce one of them by adding data. Which one
   and why? Give a dataset example where adding data does NOT reduce
   variance.
10. If your validation curve and training curve are both high and close
    together, what is wrong and what are your three best interventions?
11. Some say the test set should be a sample of the DEPLOYMENT distribution,
    not the training distribution. Formalize this and give two real
    scenarios where training and deployment distributions differ.
12. You must ship a model next week with 500 labeled samples. Compare in
    detail: simple linear model, KNN, deep net, and "ask an expert" as a
    baseline. Which do you trust and why?
13. What is the difference between a model being wrong and being uncertain?
    How do you measure each, and which matters more for a medical screener?
14. Design an experiment to prove (or disprove) that adding feature X
    improves a model - controls, splits, repeats, and how you would report
    the result without overclaiming.
15. OPEN PROBLEM: Is there a principled, universally agreed definition of
    "generalization" that covers classical ML, deep learning, and LLMs?
    Survey the definitions you know and argue which is most useful.
