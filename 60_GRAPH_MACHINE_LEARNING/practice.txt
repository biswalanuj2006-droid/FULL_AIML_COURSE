================================================================================
PRACTICE - 60 GRAPH MACHINE LEARNING
================================================================================

PART A - CONCEPTUAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. For each problem, say why a graph (not a table) is the right
   representation and what the nodes/edges are: friend suggestion;
   molecule toxicity; paper topic classification; fraud rings; code
   bug detection.
2. Adjacency vs Laplacian: what does x^T L x measure for a 0/1 signal
   x on the nodes? Why is the Laplacian called a smoothness operator?
3. Transductive vs inductive models: which graph methods can score a
   node never seen in training, and what do they require at inference?
4. Why does DeepWalk reuse the word2vec machinery? What is the "sentence"
   in a graph, and what similarity does node2vec's walk bias control?

PART B - MATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Write the GCN layer update. Show what the symmetrically normalized
   adjacency D^{-1/2} (A + I) D^{-1/2} does to a hub node of degree
   10,000 vs a leaf of degree 1 (compute the effective weighting).
6. Derive how L layers make a node's representation depend on its
   L-hop neighborhood. Bound the neighborhood size growth for a
   d-regular graph and explain over-smoothing at depth ~10+.
7. GAT: write the attention coefficient alpha_uv (with LeakyReLU and
   softmax over neighbors). What changes vs the GCN's fixed 1/sqrt(d)
   weights? What extra cost does it pay?
8. Link prediction: define the training set construction precisely
   (positive edges, negative sampling ratio) and derive why random
   splitting of an edge TABLE leaks (why time/edge splits matter).

PART C - CODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. numpy implementation of one GCN layer; run 2-layer GCN on the karate
   club graph; report classification accuracy with 10 labeled nodes vs
   an MLP on node features only.
10. Random-walk embeddings: generate walks, train skip-gram-style
    embeddings (borrow machinery from 24), and evaluate link prediction
    AUC vs random and vs common-neighbors heuristic.
11. Implement mean/max-pool GraphSAGE-style aggregation on a sampled
    neighborhood; compare against full-neighborhood GCN on a mid-size
    synthetic graph for train time and accuracy.
12. Negative sampling study for link prediction: ratios 1:1, 1:5, 1:20
    - report AUC and precision@k changes and explain the bias each
    ratio induces.

PART D - DEBUGGING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. A 2-layer GCN matches a feature-only MLP. Run the checks: giant
    component size, average degree, label homophily (do friends share
    labels?). Which check tells you the graph carries no signal?
14. Validation accuracy oscillates wildly between epochs on a GCN. What
    is the likely cause (label imbalance? high-degree variance?
    full-batch vs sampled?) and the fix?
15. Link prediction AUC is 0.99 - too good. What leakage did you
    create, and what split fixes it?
16. Deep (20-layer) GCN underperforms its 2-layer version. Explain
    over-smoothing and verify with the representation-collapse metric
    (mean pairwise cosine of node embeddings per layer).
17. New users get garbage embeddings at serving time. Which design
    choice caused this (transductive model) and what is the inductive
    replacement?

PART E - REAL WORLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18. Social network with 2B edges: design the training pipeline
    (sampling, negative sampling, parallelism, depth) and the serving
    path for "people you may know".
19. Fraud rings: why are GNNs promising for fraud detection, and what
    makes them hard in practice (label scarcity, adversarial edges,
    heterophily: fraudsters connect to look normal)?
20. (Interview) Explain message passing to someone who knows CNNs:
    "convolution over a graph = weighted average of neighbors through
    a learned matrix" - justify the analogy and name the difference
    (irregular neighborhood sizes/ordering).
21. (Interview) Graph RAG vs vector RAG: give a question where each
    wins, and the retrieval step of the graph version.
22. (Interview) Inductive vs transductive in one minute with an
    example (new paper in a citation network).

PART F - EXPERT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
23. Heterophily: construct a graph where neighbors tend to have
    OPPOSITE labels; show a GCN fails and test a fix (ego-graph
    feature separation or higher-order aggregation).
24. Scalability: compare neighbor sampling vs Cluster-GCN on a graph
    too big for full-batch; report convergence and accuracy trade-offs.
25. (Open) Expressivity: show two non-isomorphic graphs that 1-WL
    (and therefore message-passing GNNs) cannot distinguish; propose a
    feature that breaks the tie and test it on a small benchmark.
