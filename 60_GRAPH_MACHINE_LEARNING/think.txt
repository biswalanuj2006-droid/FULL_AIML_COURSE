================================================================================
THINK - 60 GRAPH MACHINE LEARNING (hard reasoning)
================================================================================
Answer with derivations or arguments. Items marked OPEN PROBLEM are
genuinely unsolved; say what evidence would settle them.

1. Prove/argue that one GCN layer computes a normalized weighted
   average of neighbor features, and that stacking L layers is a
   polynomial filter of the adjacency. What does this say about what
   the model CANNOT represent without node features?

2. Weisfeiler-Lehman: two graphs that are 1-WL indistinguishable cannot
   be told apart by any message-passing GNN with injective aggregation.
   State the limit precisely and name one architecture family that
   escapes it (higher-order / substructure counting) and its cost.

3. Over-smoothing: show that repeated application of the normalized
   adjacency drives node features toward the dominant eigenvector
   (all nodes ~equal). Derive why residual/skip connections and
   normalization delay but do not remove this, and what practical depth
   budget that implies.

4. Degree bias: a node of degree 1 aggregates one neighbor; a hub
   averages 10,000. Analyze how this skews learned representations and
   propose a normalization or sampling scheme that provably reduces the
   skew.

5. Heterophily: message passing assumes neighbors are informative
   (often same-class). For fraud (fraudsters mimic normal users' edges)
   this assumption inverts. Formalize when neighbor averaging HELPS and
   when it HURTS in terms of label correlation along edges.

6. Link prediction leakage: derive exactly why random row splits leak
   when the model's message passing uses edges whose targets are in the
   test set. Give the split that provably avoids it and its cost.

7. Scalability: full-batch GCN is memory-bound by the adjacency
   product. Compare neighbor sampling (variance: how many neighbors?),
   Cluster-GCN, and decoupled propagation (precompute A^k X) in bias /
   variance / wall-clock terms.

8. Transduction vs induction: DeepWalk needs re-training for a new node;
   GraphSAGE does not. Explain what "learning an aggregator" buys and
   the assumption it makes about how new nodes attach to the graph.

9. Graph RAG: retrieval by relationship vs by text. Give a question
   class where vector search cannot win regardless of embedding quality,
   and sketch the entity-linking failure modes that make graph
   retrieval silently wrong.

10. (OPEN PROBLEM) Expressive power vs scalability vs inductive
    generalization: no message-passing architecture simultaneously
    reaches high-order expressivity, billion-edge scale, and robust
    inductive performance. Choose the two you would optimize and
    defend the trade.

11. (OPEN PROBLEM) Robustness: an adversary adds/deletes a small number
    of edges to flip a node's classification. Why is this easy against
    GNNs (message passing amplifies perturbations)? Propose a defense
    and the attack model you would evaluate it against.

12. Design GNNs for a temporal graph (edges have timestamps, structure
    evolves). Which parts of the static stack transfer, and how do you
    keep evaluation honest when neighborhoods change over time?
