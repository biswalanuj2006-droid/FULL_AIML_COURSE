================================================================================
THINK.TXT - 16 TIME SERIES
================================================================================

1. A random split on time-series data leaks the future into the past at
   the split boundary. Construct a concrete example and show the
   optimistic bias in the validation metric.
2. Stationarity: most classical models assume it. What is stationarity,
   why does differencing help, and when is a NON-stationary model
   actually the right choice?
3. Walk-forward validation: describe the procedure, and explain why it is
   more honest than K-fold for forecasting. What is its cost and when
   would you still use K-fold?
4. A naive persistence baseline (predict last value) beats your ARIMA on
   test MSE. Interpret this result: is your model useless, or is MSE the
   wrong lens? What baseline hierarchy should every forecast be compared
   against first?
5. Seasonality with period 7 vs 365: which lags/features capture each, and
   what happens to a model trained on 6 months of daily data with yearly
   seasonality?
6. Exogenous features (weather, price) improve a demand forecast in
   training. Why might they add NOTHING in production, and how do you
   design the feature set to be robust?
7. Forecast uncertainty: point forecasts hide risk. Compare prediction
   intervals from quantile regression, conformal prediction, and
   bootstrapped residuals - what does each assume?
8. ARIMA vs gradient boosting vs LSTM on a 5-year daily series. Which
   assumptions does each make about dynamics, and how do you choose
   given n=1,800 points?
9. Concept drift: a model trained on 2024 demand fails mid-2025 because
   buying behavior changed. How do you detect the drift (statistical
   test on residuals?), and what retraining policy responds fastest?
10. Anomaly detection in series: a point 6 sigma above the rolling mean
    could be an anomaly OR a new regime. Explain the difference and how
    you would distinguish a spike from a step change.
11. Multivariate series: 50 correlated sensors forecast one target. Why
    does adding correlated inputs sometimes HURT a linear model but help
    a tree model? What preprocessing differences matter?
12. Your forecasts are systematically low during promotions you know
    about but the model does not. Is this a modeling problem or a
    data-availability problem? Argue the fix at each level.
13. OPEN PROBLEM: forecasting evaluation under non-stationarity has no
    settled protocol (fixed origin vs rolling origin, metric choice,
    significance across horizons). Design the evaluation you would
    standardize on and defend it.
