================================================================================
REAL PROBLEM - 16 TIME SERIES
================================================================================

PROBLEM A: "My demand forecast is 20% better in backtests than in real
life. Every Monday the model under-predicts."
WHY IT HAPPENS (diagnose in order)
  1. Backtest used a random split -> temporal leakage inflated scores.
  2. Weekly seasonality not modeled: Monday is systematically different
     and the model learned the average.
  3. Real life has holidays/promotions that never appeared in training.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Switch to walk-forward validation and re-measure (expect the gap
     to shrink).
  2. Add day-of-week + holiday + promo features; verify Monday error
     drops.
  3. Keep a naive/persistence baseline: if your model cannot beat
     "same day last week", the signal work is not done.

PROBLEM B: "Forecast looks fine in aggregate but misses every spike."
WHY: MSE/RMSE training optimizes the average; spikes are rare so they
barely move the loss, and the model learns to ignore them.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Inspect errors by magnitude bucket - confirm spikes dominate the
     business impact but not the loss.
  2. Train with quantile loss at high quantiles (q=0.9) if you need
     upper-bound estimates, or add spike-specific features (events,
     leading indicators).
  3. Report pinball loss and coverage of prediction intervals, not just
     RMSE.

PROBLEM C: "The model degrades 3 months after each retrain."
WHY: concept drift (buying behavior, price sensitivity, competitors).
The retraining cadence is fixed; drift speed varies.
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Monitor residuals: rolling mean of (actual - prediction). When it
     moves > k*std persistently, that is drift, not noise.
  2. Retrain when drift is detected (threshold on the drift metric),
     not on a calendar.
  3. Keep the last N good models: if a retrain performs worse, roll
     back automatically (champion/challenger with an alert).

PROBLEM D: "Sales spike every Friday but my model flattens them."
WHY: features (day-of-week) exist, but the model is underfit or the
tree splits cannot express the interaction (weekday x product x promo).
HOW TO SOLVE
  1. Confirm the signal exists: group mean by day-of-week - is Friday
     actually higher? If flat in data, it is a data problem.
  2. Add interaction features and raise model capacity.
  3. Verify on a Friday-only holdout slice - evaluate where the
     business actually cares.
