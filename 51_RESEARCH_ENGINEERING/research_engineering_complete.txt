LEARNING OBJECTIVES
-------------------
After this module you will be able to:
- Read and understand ML research papers
- Reimplement papers from descriptions

- Write technical research reports

================================================================================
1. HOW TO READ RESEARCH PAPERS
================================================================================

FIRST PASS (5 minutes)
-----------------------
- Read title, abstract, introduction
- Look at figures and tables
- Read conclusion
- Get the "big picture"

SECOND PASS (30 minutes)
-------------------------
- Read methods section carefully
- Understand the architecture/approach
- Note key equations
- Skip complex proofs for now

THIRD PASS (1-2 hours)
-----------------------
- Understand every equation
- Reproduce figures mentally
- Identify assumptions and limitations
- Think about improvements

================================================================================
2. PAPER IMPLEMENTATION PROCESS
================================================================================

1. UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM
   What is the paper trying to solve?

2. UNDERSTAND THE APPROACH
   What is their proposed solution?

3. IDENTIFY KEY COMPONENTS
   What are the novel contributions?

4. IMPLEMENT COMPONENT BY COMPONENT
   Build each part separately, test it.

5. COMBINE AND TEST
   Put it all together, debug.

6. COMPARE WITH BASELINE
   Does it match paper results?

7. EXPERIMENT
   Try variations, ablations.

================================================================================
3. EXPERIMENT DESIGN
================================================================================

HYPOTHESIS FORMATION
---------------------
"A good hypothesis is specific, testable, and falsifiable."

Example:
"Weak hypothesis: Deep learning is good for NLP."
"Strong hypothesis: Fine-tuning BERT with domain-specific data
improves sentiment analysis accuracy by >5% on financial text."

BASELINES
---------
Always compare against:
- Simple baseline (random, majority class)
- Previous state-of-the-art
- Your proposed method

ABLATION STUDIES
----------------
Systematically remove components to understand their contribution.
Example:
- Full model: 92% accuracy
- Without attention: 87% (attention helps +5%)
- Without pretraining: 84% (pretraining helps +8%)
- Without regularization: 85% (regularization helps +7%)

STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE
-------------------------
Never compare two numbers without statistical testing.

from scipy import stats

# Paired t-test
t_stat, p_value = stats.ttest_rel(accuracies_model_a, accuracies_model_b)
print(f"p-value: {p_value:.4f}")
# p < 0.05 → significant difference

# For multiple runs
import numpy as np
model_a_scores = [run_model_a(seed) for seed in range(10)]
model_b_scores = [run_model_b(seed) for seed in range(10)]
print(f"Model A: {np.mean(model_a_scores):.4f} ± {np.std(model_a_scores):.4f}")
print(f"Model B: {np.mean(model_b_scores):.4f} ± {np.std(model_b_scores):.4f}")

5. TECHNICAL WRITING
================================================================================

RESEARCH REPORT STRUCTURE
---------------------------
1. Title and Abstract
2. Introduction (problem, motivation, contribution)
3. Related Work
4. Method (detailed approach)
5. Experiments (setup, results, analysis)
6. Conclusion

WRITING RULES
--------------
- Be precise: "Model A achieved 94.2% accuracy" not "Model A is good"
- Show tables, not just prose
- Include ablation studies
- Discuss limitations honestly
- Cite related work properly
