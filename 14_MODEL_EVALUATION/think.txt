THINK.TXT - 14 MODEL EVALUATION
================================================================================

1. You tuned hyperparameters on the validation set 200 times. Explain why
   the validation score is now optimistic and why the test set is your
   only honest number - and what you do when you only have one dataset.
2. Accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC: for a fraud model
   where positives cost $50 and negatives cost $1, derive which metric
   matches the economics and why.
3. ROC-AUC is 0.95, PR-AUC is 0.3, on the same model. Reconcile these.
   Which do you report to the business and why?
4. K-fold CV assumes exchangeable rows. Give three datasets where this
   fails (groups, time, duplicates) and the correct CV variant for each.
5. Nested CV: outer loop estimates performance, inner loop tunes.
   Explain the bias in "tune then measure on the same fold" and why
   nested CV fixes it, including its cost.
6. Class imbalance and F1: is F1 macro or weighted more honest for a
   99:1 problem? What does each answer and who is asking?
7. Your model's ROC curve is a perfect step: AUC 1.0. Is the model
   perfect? What could produce this artifact and how do you check?
8. Calibration: the model says 0.8 for 100 predictions and 82 are
   positive. Is it calibrated? How do you measure calibration beyond
   this (ECE, reliability diagram)?
9. Statistical comparison: Model A beats Model B by 0.01 ROC-AUC on one
   test set. Run the argument for why you should not deploy A, including
   the test of significance you would use and its assumptions.
10. Learning curves: train error and val error converge high. What do you
    do? Both low with a gap? Only train error low? Map each pattern to
    its remedy.
11. Regression: RMSE is dominated by a handful of huge errors; MAE hides
    them. Give the business scenario for each being the "correct" choice
    and a third metric that balances both.
12. You evaluate on the same test set every week for a year while
    retraining monthly. Explain test-set overfitting over time and how
    rolling/refreshed test sets fix it.
13. Cost-sensitive evaluation: define expected cost of a classifier given
    a confusion matrix and costs c_FP, c_FN, c_TP, c_TN. Derive the
    optimal operating point on the ROC curve.
14. OPEN PROBLEM: point estimates hide uncertainty. Design an evaluation
    report that reports a model's performance with honest uncertainty
    (intervals, repeated splits) and discuss what remains unsolved in
    comparing such intervals.
