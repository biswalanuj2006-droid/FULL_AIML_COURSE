================================================================================
THINK - 61 REINFORCEMENT LEARNING (hard reasoning)
================================================================================
Answer with derivations or arguments. Items marked OPEN PROBLEM are
genuinely unsolved; say what evidence would settle them.

1. Show that Q-learning's update is a stochastic approximation to the
   Bellman optimality operator and derive the conditions (step sizes,
   exploration) under which tabular Q-learning converges to Q*. Where
   does that convergence proof break when Q is a neural net?

2. The deadly triad: function approximation + bootstrapping + off-policy
   can diverge. Explain each term's role in the divergence mechanism and
   why target networks + replay are partial, not complete, fixes.

3. Policy gradient: the score-function estimator has variance growing
   with horizon. Derive why subtracting a state-dependent baseline does
   not change the expectation, and derive the OPTIMAL baseline
   b*(s) = E[||grad log pi||^2 G] / E[||grad log pi||^2]. Why is the
   value function a practical approximation to it?

4. REINFORCE with return-as-weight credits every action by the WHOLE
   episode return. Explain credit assignment failure with a 100-step
   episode where only step 90 was good, and how advantage/GAE fixes it
   by discounting future credit.

5. PPO clip: write the clipped surrogate and prove/argue that it is a
   lower bound on the (unclipped) surrogate when the ratio moves away
   from 1 - the "conservative policy iteration" guarantee. What does the
   bound cost you (bias, sample reuse)?

6. Reward hacking is the RL analog of Goodhart's law. Formalize: the
   agent maximizes the measured reward R_hat which is a proxy for true
   objective R*. Give conditions under which optimizing R_hat strictly
   decreases R*, and the mitigation hierarchy (better reward, shaping,
   constrained RL, monitoring).

7. Exploration: epsilon-greedy explores uniformly; count-based bonus
   explores by novelty. Compare their regret on a sparse-reward
   environment and derive why intrinsic motivation can change the
   effective objective (the "noisy TV" problem).

8. RLHF: the reward model r_phi is itself learned from human
   preferences and can be gamed. Why does the KL-to-reference penalty in
   the PPO objective exist, and what failure would you see if it were
   zero? What does "reward model overoptimization" look like on the
   evaluation curves?

9. Off-policy evaluation: you have logs from policy A and want to
   estimate policy B's value. Write the importance-sampling estimator
   and explain its variance explosion on long horizons; then argue why
   this makes reliable offline RL evaluation an open problem.

10. (OPEN PROBLEM) Sample efficiency: RL needs orders of magnitude more
    interaction than supervised learning needs labels. State the gap
    precisely (regret vs sample complexity bounds) and what a
    breakthrough result would have to demonstrate to change practice.

11. (OPEN PROBLEM) Reward specification: defining rewards that induce
    the intended behavior remains unsolved - inverse RL learns rewards
    from demonstrations but is ill-posed (many rewards explain the same
    behavior). Propose an evaluation that would convince you a new
    reward-learning method actually recovered the true objective.

12. Design: an LLM agent with tools is trained by RL to book flights.
    Sketch the MDP (what is a state? an action?), where the reward
    comes from, the safety constraints you must add, and which parts of
    the pipeline you would trust only with human oversight.
