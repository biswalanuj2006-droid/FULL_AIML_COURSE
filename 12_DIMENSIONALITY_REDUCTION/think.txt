THINK.TXT - 12 DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION

1. PCA maximizes variance of projections. Derive why the top eigenvector
   of the covariance matrix is the solution, and state the implicit
   Gaussian assumption.
2. PCA is scale-dependent. Show a 2-feature example where not
   standardizing reverses the first component, and argue when raw PCA
   (without scaling) is actually correct.
3. Why does PCA fail on data lying on a curved manifold (e.g., a
   "swiss roll")? What does t-SNE/UMAP assume instead?
4. Explained variance: 95% threshold selects k components. What does the
   remaining 5% contain that could still matter for a classifier? Give a
   concrete case where low-variance directions are the signal.
5. t-SNE preserves LOCAL structure and often produces visually separate
   clusters that do not exist. How do you tell a real cluster from a
   t-SNE artifact, and what do perplexity and early exaggeration do?
6. UMAP vs t-SNE vs PCA: you must embed 1M vectors for visualization.
   Compare runtime, global vs local structure, and reproducibility.
7. PCA for compression vs PCA for noise reduction: reconstructing faces
   from 50 components vs using them as classifier features - what is
   being lost and gained in each?
8. Kernel PCA implicitly maps to a high-dimensional space. How can
   "going higher" help reduce dimensionality, and what does the kernel
   choice encode about the data?
9. NMF produces non-negative parts (e.g., topics, facial features).
   Derive why non-negativity changes interpretability, and when you would
   prefer NMF over PCA.
10. You apply PCA inside a pipeline for a classifier. Where exactly must
    the PCA fit happen to avoid leakage, and what happens to your CV
    score if you fit it on all data first?
11. Random Projection is distribution-free and fast. Explain the
    Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma intuition: how can random projections
    preserve distances, and when would you choose it over PCA?
12. OPEN PROBLEM: all linear and manifold methods assume SOME structure
    (linear subspace, manifold, cluster). Is there a principled way to
    choose the structure assumption from data? Discuss current
    approaches and their limits.
