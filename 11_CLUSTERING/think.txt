THINK.TXT - 11 CLUSTERING

1. Clustering is "unsupervised" yet every algorithm optimizes SOMETHING.
   State the objective of K-Means, DBSCAN, hierarchical (Ward), and GMM,
   and explain how choosing an algorithm is choosing an assumption.
2. K-Means minimizes within-cluster variance, which is equivalent to
   assuming spherical Gaussian clusters. What happens with elongated
   clusters and why does standardization not fully fix it?
3. The elbow method is subjective. Derive a more principled k-selection
   criterion (silhouette, gap statistic) and explain when it still fails.
4. Two moons dataset: K-Means fails, spectral clustering succeeds. Explain
   what "cluster" means in each and why the geometry matters.
5. DBSCAN has no "cluster center". What does eps control exactly, and
   what is the failure mode when clusters have very different densities?
   (This is why HDBSCAN exists - explain.)
6. You cluster customers and get 5 groups, but a stakeholder says group 2
   is "not actionable". What does actionability mean for clustering
   validation, and how does it conflict with purely internal metrics?
7. Clustering stability: run K-Means twice with different seeds and get
   different labels. How do you measure stability and what does
   instability tell you about the data?
8. GMM gives soft assignments - a probability per cluster. When would
   those probabilities be more useful than hard labels, and what
   assumption about the data-generating process do they make?
9. Scale matters: clustering on raw vs standardized vs PCA-transformed
   features can give entirely different clusters. Construct a rule for
   when each is defensible.
10. How do you know a clustering is "good" when you have NO ground truth?
    Enumerate the internal metrics, what each assumes, and their failure
    modes (silhouette loves convex clusters - why?).
11. Anomaly detection as clustering: how do isolation forest, one-class
    SVM, and GMM's density threshold each define "normal"? Which is best
    when anomalies are rare and heterogeneous?
12. OPEN PROBLEM: the "right number of clusters" has no objective answer
    without a downstream task. Formalize clustering as a decision problem
    with a utility function and discuss what is still unsolved.
