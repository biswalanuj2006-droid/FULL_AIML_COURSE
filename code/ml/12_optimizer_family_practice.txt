================================================================================
PRACTICE: OPTIMIZER FAMILY (SGD -> momentum -> AdaGrad -> RMSProp -> Adam)
================================================================================
Companion: code/ml/gradient_descent_from_scratch.py (batch/SGD/mini-batch/
momentum) and 05_MATHEMATICS/06_OPTIMIZATION. This set makes you
implement the full adaptive family and DEBUG the differences empirically.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 - CONCEPTUAL (write answers first, no code)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Write the update rule for vanilla SGD, SGD+momentum, Nesterov
   momentum, AdaGrad, RMSProp, and Adam, in a consistent notation.
   Circle: every place a gradient appears, every place a learning rate
   appears, and every place a bias correction appears.
2. AdaGrad divides by the accumulated SUM of squared gradients. Why does
   that make the effective step size vanish over time, and why is that
   "adaptive" (rare features get bigger steps)?
3. RMSProp replaces the sum with an EXPONENTIAL MOVING AVERAGE. Write the
   decay. Why does this fix AdaGrad's vanishing problem?
4. Adam = RMSProp + momentum + bias correction. What exactly does the
   bias correction fix, and why does it matter most in the FIRST steps?
5. AdamW differs from Adam in ONE place. Where, and what failure of
   L2-regularized Adam does it fix? (Think: what happens to weight decay
   when it is implemented as an L2 penalty and the effective step size
   is normalized?)
6. Which optimizer converges fastest in the first 100 steps on a
   noisy sparse problem? Which generalizes worst in the long run, and
   what is the empirical story on transformers (AdamW) vs CNNs (SGD+
   momentum often wins on accuracy)?
7. What does the learning rate MEAN in Adam vs SGD? (SGD: actual step
   size in weight space. Adam: a fraction of the parameter's own scale -
   which is why 3e-4 "just works" across tasks.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 - NUMERICAL (by hand on one step)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. One weight w = 2.0, gradient g = -0.5, lr = 0.1, momentum beta = 0.9,
   velocity v = 0 initially.
   a) SGD step: what is w after one update?
   b) Momentum: what is v1 and w after one update?
   c) Show that with constant gradient -0.5 for 10 steps, momentum's
      step size approaches g/(1-beta) * lr = -5 * lr asymptotically,
      while SGD's total movement after 10 steps is 10 * lr * 0.5.
9. Adam first step: m = 0, v = 0, g1 = -0.5, lr = 3e-4, beta1 = 0.9,
   beta2 = 0.999, eps = 1e-8.
   a) Compute m1, v1 (unbiased) and the corrected m1_hat, v1_hat.
   b) Compute the update. What would the update be WITHOUT bias
      correction? (This is why the first Adam step is not tiny.)
10. AdaGrad: w = 1.0, lr = 0.1, accumulate G. After gradients g1 = 1.0,
    g2 = 0.01, what are the two step sizes? Show the second step is
    roughly 10x smaller relative to the first in step-size-per-unit-
    gradient terms.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 - CODE (implement and verify each claim)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. Implement all six optimizers as functions that update a numpy
    parameter array given a gradient, keeping their internal state.
    Verify your updates by hand against PART 2 answers.
12. Convergence sanity: minimize f(w) = ||X w - y||^2 on a random
    well-conditioned problem (n=200, d=10). All six should converge to
    the same optimum; report steps-to-1e-6-loss for each at a fair lr.
13. The sparse-gradient test: make 95% of gradient entries zero. Compare
    steps taken by AdaGrad/RMSProp/Adam vs SGD on the rare coordinates.
    Confirm: adaptive methods move the rare coordinates faster.
14. The ill-conditioned test: f(w) = 100 w1^2 + w2^2 (a banana-shaped
    valley). SGD+momentum zigzags; which method handles it best and
    why? Plot the trajectories.
15. Numerical check (this is the debugging part): implement Adam and
    VERIFY that the m and v updates use the SAME gradient at step t
    (a classic bug updates v with the momentum-corrected gradient, or
    updates m before computing the correction). Write the buggy
    version, show it converges slower or diverges, then fix it.
16. AdamW: implement Adam with L2 penalty added to the loss vs AdamW
    (decay applied directly to weights). Train a tiny MLP with both at
    identical lr and weight decay; measure final train loss and the
    actual L2 norm of the weights. They are NOT equivalent when the
    adaptive step size varies - show it.
17. lr schedule interplay: take Adam at lr=3e-4 with (a) fixed lr,
    (b) cosine decay, (c) linear warmup + decay, on a small CNN-ish
    problem (or MLP on MNIST via code/dl examples). Report final loss;
    explain why warmup matters for Adam (huge early effective steps on
    rare coordinates).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 4 - DEBUGGING (each scenario has a planted cause)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18. Loss explodes to NaN at step 50 with Adam at lr = 1e-3 but trains
    fine at 1e-4. Name two candidate causes and the diagnostic that
    distinguishes them (gradient norm log vs loss log).
19. Adam reaches loss 0.01 and stops improving; SGD+momentum from the
    same start reaches 0.005 but needs 10x more steps. What does this
    say about the two optimizers' final neighborhoods, and how do you
    exploit it in practice (optimizer switching / fine-tuning)?
20. Your per-parameter update is fine in fp64 but NaN in fp32 after
    100k steps with Adam. Which term is most likely overflowing, and
    where do you put the eps (hint: eps in the sqrt, not after)?
21. Momentum with beta=0.99 overshoots on a convex quadratic and
    oscillates; beta=0.9 is fine. Derive the step-size bound that
    explains it, then confirm numerically.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 5 - REAL-WORLD / INTERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
22. You are fine-tuning a transformer. Default AdamW lr? Why not SGD?
    What happens if you forget the bias correction in the first 10
    steps?
23. You are training a CNN on images with a batch of 128 on one GPU.
    Your colleague claims Adam always beats SGD here. What experiment
    settles it, and what is the common empirical outcome after a full
    LR schedule?
24. Explain to a non-expert why "the optimizer" is a hyperparameter you
    must tune together with the learning rate, not separately.
25. (Open) When would you deliberately choose SGD+momentum over Adam
    even though Adam converges faster in loss? Give a concrete
    generalization argument and the test that would support it.
